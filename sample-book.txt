Every morning, Lily the young gardener walked through the old stone gate of the sunflower farm, her straw hat bobbing above the golden rows. She carried a tin watering can that had belonged to her grandmother, and she sang to the flowers as she worked.

One day she found a small, muddy crow sitting under the big oak, shivering. She named him Barnaby and fed him breadcrumbs from her pocket. Barnaby followed her everywhere after that, riding on her shoulder while she planted seeds and chasing the butterflies between the rows.

That autumn a frost came early and threatened the whole harvest. Lily worked all night by lantern light, and Barnaby flew to the neighbouring farms to call for help. By dawn, every farmer in the valley stood in the field with a lantern of their own, saving the sunflowers before the sun rose.

When spring came again, the field was fuller than ever, and Barnaby had learned to whistle the song Lily sang. The children of the valley came to the farm every day to hear the whistling crow and to help the young gardener who never gave up.
